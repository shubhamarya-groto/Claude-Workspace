# Desktop Audit – Go Find Build x Groto (landing pages)

19 issues. Source: `Source files/Audit_Go_find_Build_X_Groto_-_Desktop_Audit.csv`. Sorted by criticality.

| # | Criticality | Issue Type | Problem | Proposed Solution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical | Typography | No typographic hierarchy system — all weights feel the same. Headings, subheadings, body copy, and captions compete for attention with no clear weight or size ladder. Users can't scan the page to understand priority. | Establish a 4-level type scale: H1, H2, H3, body. Apply consistently across both landing pages. Increase contrast between heading weight and body weight. |
| 2 | Critical | Visual Consistency | Two completely different visual languages across the two landing pages. The Companies page and Job Seekers page feel like two separate websites with different hero styles, card types, and icon treatments. | Create a shared design system: a consistent hero template, shared card component, uniform icon set, and identical section-to-section rhythm. Both pages should feel like siblings. |
| 3 | Critical | CTA Design | Primary CTAs are inconsistent in shape, size, color, and labeling. At least 4 different CTA button styles are used across the site for the same action, breaking brand trust and user confidence. | Define exactly 2 CTA variants: Primary and Secondary. Use the same label for the same action everywhere. Remove the mixed angled/pill shapes. |
| 4 | High | Color Usage | Orange background sections have poor text contrast approaching WCAG AA failure. Orange is overused — appearing as background fills, button fills, icon fills, and accent text simultaneously, diluting brand impact. | Reserve orange exclusively for CTA buttons and 1–2 key accent highlights. Use navy or white for large section backgrounds. |
| 5 | High | Layout / Spacing | Section spacing is uneven — sections feel crammed then suddenly very spaced out. The page lacks a consistent vertical rhythm, making it feel unplanned. 'How it works' sits almost directly under the hero while the stats section has excessive whitespace above. | Define a spacing system: section padding, sub-section. Apply uniformly. Use an 8px base grid. The vertical breathing room should feel identical across every section. |
| 6 | High | Icon Design | Mixed icon styles — emoji, flat illustration, outlined, and photographic circles coexist on the same pages. This visual noise signals a lack of design control and looks amateur despite the strong photography. | Choose one icon system. Remove all emoji from non-emoji contexts. Reserve photo-circles for testimonials or team sections only. |
| 7 | High | Photography | Hero photo cropping cuts key subjects in uncomfortable ways. In the main hero, the excavator arm is cropped at the edge accidentally. In the companies page hero, the workers are cut off at the waist wasting the central subject. | Define a hero image art direction guide: subjects framed with 20% breathing room on all sides. Avoid cropping at joints. Use photo positions that leave the left 35% of the frame open for text overlay. |
| 8 | High | Pricing Section | Pricing card design has no visual hierarchy or recommended tier highlight. All three tiers (Candidate $99 / Skilled $199 / Expert $399) look identical — same border, same weight, same size. Users can't quickly determine which tier to choose. | Elevate the middle tier ('Skilled Applicant') with a Most Popular badge, slightly larger card, and a navy border. Add checkmark lists of what each tier includes. Use background color shift for elevation. |
| 9 | High | Navigation | No active state or hover indicator on nav links. 'Companies' and 'Job Seekers' links have no underline, no current-page indicator, and no visible hover state, leaving users unsure of their location. | Add an active underline indicator on the current page link. Add a subtle hover color change. Ensure the active page is always highlighted in the nav. |
| 10 | Medium | How It Works Section | How it works' section feels incomplete — only 1 of 3 steps is visually active. 'Post your jobs' and 'Review and hire' appear as plain gray text with no visual treatment. The 3-step flow is the most important conversion argument but looks unfinished. | Give each step an icon, number badge, and consistent active/inactive state. Connect steps with a vertical timeline line. Animate on scroll or allow tab-switching to reveal product screenshots for each step. |
| 11 | Medium | Color / Backgrounds | Background color ramp has too many distinct backgrounds per page — 7 different background treatments visible while scrolling. Each transition feels jarring and disconnects sections visually. | Limit to 3 backgrounds. Use in a predictable alternating rhythm. Remove the orange and light blue background sections. |
| 12 | Medium | Decorative Elements | Oversized gear watermarks feel dated and add visual noise. Large semi-transparent gear icons appear in the orange section and beige job-cards section. The gear motif is a late-2010s design pattern that reads as clutter. | Remove the large watermark gears entirely from section backgrounds. If using the gear as a brand element, confine it to the logo and favicon only. Replace with subtle geometric line patterns or remove decorative backgrounds. |
| 13 | Medium | Card Design | Job listing cards in the floating mockup use too many font sizes and truncate inconsistently. The stacked/tilted card layout makes all three cards partially unreadable and the descriptions are cut off mid-sentence. | Use a horizontal card list layout instead of overlapping. Standardize card anatomy: company name, job title, location + salary in a row, tags, 2-line description max, CTA button. Align truncation at exactly 2 lines. |
| 14 | Medium | Stats Section | Stats section numbers lack visual anchoring and supporting text is too long. The 4 stat blocks have 3–4 line descriptions and citation footnotes under each, making what should be a punchy visual moment into 30 seconds of reading. | Reduce each stat description to 1 bold sentence (max 12 words). Move citations to a single footnote line at the bottom. Add a small icon above each stat number. The section should feel like a 3-second scan. |
| 15 | Medium | Icon / Content Quality | Pain-point cards use stock emoji icons that undermine credibility. The 'We know the struggle' section uses calendar, wrench, and blue diamond emoji as icons. The diamond emoji has no semantic relationship to 'skilled tradespeople are hard to find.' | Replace with consistent outlined construction-trade icons: a stopwatch for time/cost, a hard-hat for finding talent, a wrench for skilled trades. Keep the orange header strip but replace emoji with 32px SVG icons in white. |
| 16 | Medium | Photo Treatment | Circular photo collage lacks visual logic and depth hierarchy. The 'Let's change the game' section uses 3 circular worker photos connected by blue lines suggesting a network diagram, but the lines connect to nothing meaningful. | Either: (a) replace with 3 clean rectangular cards with role labels under each photo, or (b) use the network metaphor intentionally — label each node and have lines connect to a central 'GoFindBuild' hub to visualize matching. |
| 17 | Low | Footer | Footer is near-empty and missing standard trust links. Only the copyright text, logo, and social icons are present. No links to Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, or Contact — standard trust signals expected before sign-up. | Add a minimal 3-column footer: Column 1 — logo + tagline. Column 2 — Company links (About, Blog, Contact). Column 3 — Legal (Privacy, Terms, Accessibility). Keep clean on the dark navy background. |
| 18 | Low | Logo / Branding | Logo lockup has inconsistent kerning between icon and wordmark. The gear icon sits slightly too far from the wordmark, and the GO/FIND/BUILD color split looks like three separate words rather than a unified brand name. | Tighten the icon-to-wordmark gap. Consider a unified wordmark treatment with slightly tightened letter-spacing (−0.02em) to improve cohesion at nav bar size. |
| 19 | Low | Microcopy / Typography | Section subtitle copy is too long and generic, reducing visual hierarchy. Body paragraphs start immediately under headings with no breathing room, killing the heading's impact. Key section copy runs over recommended 20-word limit. | Add 16px gap between heading and body text. Limit section subtitles to 2 lines / 20 words max. Use a slightly larger font size for the subtitle (18px) to create a clear 3-level hierarchy: heading → subtitle → body. |
